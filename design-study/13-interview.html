Interview kit: from entry level to senior and architect

Seniors are not judged on knowing more tools. They are judged on trade-offs, failure thinking, numbers and clear communication.

What changes from junior to senior to architect

LevelAnswers…LMS example
Junior"How do I implement it?""Use a unique index."
Senior"Why this, what are the trade-offs, what fails?""A unique day table makes overlap impossible even under concurrency; the price is two rows a working day and a copied EmployeeId guarded by a composite FK."
Architect"What does this mean for teams, cost, evolution and risk?""Modular monolith now; schemas mark future service borders; outbox lets us add RabbitMQ without changing the request path; revisit at X."

Paper exercises (do them without looking)

  1. Draw the LMS containers and trace the apply request (10 min).
  2. Draw the six core tables with PKs, FKs and the three DB-enforced rules (15 min).
  3. Write the API calls for preview, submit, list, inbox, approve, cancel with headers (15 min).
  4. Draw the state machine and list who may make each move (5 min).
  5. Pick a new system (library, clinic, parking) and run the 7-step method in 30 minutes.
