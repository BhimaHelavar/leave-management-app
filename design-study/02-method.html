The design method: seven steps for any system

Interviewers do not want a perfect answer; they want to see a repeatable way of thinking. Use this order every time. Click a step to see it applied to the LMS.

The seven steps in detail

StepDo thisSay out loudTime in a 45-minute interview
1 ClarifyAsk 5 questions: users, scale, must-haves, out-of-scope, constraints"Let me confirm scope before I draw."5 min
2 RequirementsWrite FR (top 5) and NFR (numbers)"The hard requirement is never negative balance."5 min
3 EstimateUsers → requests per second → storage"This is about 20 writes a day, so no need to shard."3 min
4 InterfaceList endpoints or events, with errors"Approve is an action, not a PATCH of status."5 min
5 Data modelEntities, keys, constraints, indexes"The database must refuse overlap itself."8 min
6 HLDBoxes and arrows, one request traced"Request saved with its outbox row in one transaction."8 min
7 Deep dive2 hard problems, trade-offs, failure modes"If the worker crashes the row is still there."11 min
Why this order?Requirements and numbers decide the shape. People who start with "I'll use Kafka and microservices" fail because they chose a tool before knowing the problem. Your LMS decisions (modular monolith, SQL Server, outbox) came from steps 1 to 3.

Five questions to ask in step 1 (copy these)

  1. Who are the users and roles, and how many?
  2. What are the 3 to 5 must-have actions?
  3. What is the scale now and in 2 years?
  4. What must never go wrong? (money, double booking, data loss)
  5. What is out of scope, and what are the constraints (budget, team, stack)?

Trade-off sentence (use it for every decision)

I chose [A] over [B] because [requirement/number].
The cost is [price]; I accept it because [reason].
I would revisit when [trigger].

LMS example: "I chose a modular monolith over microservices because one team and 20 writes a day need no independent scaling. The cost is that module borders rely on discipline, not the network. I would revisit if one module needed its own release cycle or scale."
