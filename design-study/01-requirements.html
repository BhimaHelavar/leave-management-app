Requirements: the 100 % of what the LMS must do

Every design decision traces back to a requirement. Learn to read, rank and test requirements, and you can design any system.

What a requirement is (and the two kinds)

  • Business requirement: a need of the business, in business words. "HR cannot prove who approved what."
  • Functional requirement (FR): something the system does. "A manager can approve or reject with a comment."
  • Non-functional requirement (NFR): how well it does it: speed, uptime, security, privacy. "p95 under 300 ms."
  • Constraint: a limit you cannot change. "Free tiers only; 8 to 10 hours a week."
Rule of thumbIf you cannot write a test for it, it is not a requirement yet. "Fast" fails; "p95 under 300 ms at 50 users" passes.

The business problem

Leave is tracked in email and spreadsheets, so balances are wrong, approvals stall and HR cannot prove who approved what. Objectives: approvals in under one working day, balance errors near zero, full audit trail. Company: Acme India Pvt Ltd, about 300 employees (Bengaluru, Pune, one non-India office); India-first rules; leave year 1 January to 31 December. MVP: one location (Bengaluru, Asia/Kolkata).

User stories and acceptance criteria

A user story is As a [role] I want [goal] so that [value] with Given / When / Then tests. The MVP has 12 stories in Jira epic SCRUM-7; examples:

StoryOne acceptance test
US-01 Sign in and see only what my role allows (BR-01)Given I am an Employee, when I call an Admin-only endpoint, then I get 403 and the attempt is audited
US-03 Apply for leave, counting working days (BR-03)Given Friday to Tuesday with a Monday holiday, when I preview, then 2 working days are counted
US-05 Block invalid requestsGiven I already have leave on that day, when I submit, then 409 leave_overlap
US-07 Approve or rejectGiven a rejection without a comment, then it is refused (422 comment_required)

Your turn: three habits

  1. For every feature ask: who needs it, why (business need), how well (NFR), what if it fails?
  2. Write a rule as a test sentence before you design it.
  3. Always say what you leave out and why ("payroll integration is a Won't for the MVP"). That is how seniors show judgement.
